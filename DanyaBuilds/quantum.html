True Quantum Random Number Generator (QRNG)

10-Qubit Circuit Design, Quantum Gate Biasing, and Real IBM Quantum Hardware Execution

Qiskit Implementation

Uniform & Biased Circuits with Hardware Verification

Classical pseudorandom number generators (PRNGs) are deterministic and mathematically vulnerable to seed prediction. Quantum mechanical superposition and measurement collapse provide the only mechanism for generating true, non-deterministic random numbers.

Developed under Prof. Rodrigo Pasti, this project implements a complete QRNG capable of generating random integers from 0 to 1000 across two distinct operational regimes, executed both in the Qiskit Aer simulator and on real IBM Quantum superconducting processors:

  • Uniform Scenario (RNG_uniforme.py): Applies Hadamard ($H$) gates across 10 qubits to generate an equal superposition: $$\lvert \psi \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1024}} \sum_{x=0}^{1023} \lvert x \rangle$$ Upon measurement, 10-bit binary strings are converted to integers $[0, 1000]$ with rejection sampling, achieving an acceptance rate of 97.3% to 97.7% and a mean value centered precisely at 494.98 – 506.40.
  • Biased Scenario (RNG_biased.py): Implements quantum phase rotations using $S$, $T$, and parameter-tuned $R_z(\theta - 3\pi/4)$ gates where $\theta = 2\arcsin(\sqrt{0.80})$. The interference skews the probability amplitudes toward $\lvert 1 \rangle$, shifting the resulting integer distribution to an average of 701.56 – 720.60 (with minimum values starting above 38).
QISKIT // IBM_QUANTUM.PY SAMPLER_V2
# Biased Circuit Gate Sequence on 10 Qubits
BIAS = 0.80
theta = 2 * math.asin(math.sqrt(BIAS))
qc = QuantumCircuit(10)

for q in range(10):
    qc.h(q)
    qc.s(q)
    qc.t(q)
    qc.rz(theta - 3 * math.pi / 4, q)
    qc.h(q)

qc.measure_all()

Post-Quantum Cryptography & The Quantum Horizon

Algorithmic complexity, Shor's threat, and lattice-based transition standards

Cryptographic Impact

Shor's & Grover's Algorithms

Shor's polynomial-time algorithm for prime factorization and discrete logarithms completely breaks RSA, Diffie-Hellman, and Elliptic Curve Cryptography. Grover's search algorithm quadratically accelerates brute-force key searches, necessitating 256-bit symmetric keys (e.g. AES-256) to maintain 128-bit quantum security margins.

COMPLEXITY_ANALYSIS QUANTUM_ADVANTAGE
Classical Factoring: exp(O(n^(1/3)))
Shor's Algorithm: O(n^3) polynomial time
Symmetric Key Search: O(2^n) → O(2^(n/2)) (Grover)

Next-Gen Standards

Lattice Cryptography (NIST FIPS 203)

Transitioning to post-quantum key encapsulation mechanisms (ML-KEM / Kyber) based on the hardness of the Learning With Errors (LWE) problem over module lattices, ensuring cryptographic security even in the presence of fault-tolerant quantum computers.
